Swipe the media viewer away. At normal size, drag the image up or down with a finger or the mouse: the image follows, and the dark backdrop fades as it goes. Let go far enough, or with a quick flick, and the viewer closes and focus goes back to the thumbnail; a short drag springs the image back. Zoomed in, a drag still pans, and a second finger turns a swipe into a pinch. With reduced motion the image does not spring or fly out: the viewer just closes or snaps back. A long image description now scrolls on its own, and is a tab stop when it does. If the reader turns reduced transparency on or off while the viewer is open, the browser's bars follow the new dim. On a Mac, Safari's toolbar also takes the viewer's dim while it is open.
